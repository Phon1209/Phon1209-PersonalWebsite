WCAG or Web Content Accessibility Guideline is a standard of making the content accessible. From my understanding, it’s just a set of criterion that is a baseline and bare minimum that would make web a little bit more accessible; basically just to comply with legal A11y requirement.

<aside> 💡

Note

To make it truly accessible, you would need to understand your user; not just following guideline.

</aside>

There’s 2 major versions of WCAG and 2 minor versions as well as the incomplete draft (WCAG 3.0) that encompass all part of development process: Design, Content Creation, and Coding.

- **WCAG 1.0**: Only target HTML content; not really applicable anymore since we have lots of other type of information.
    
- **WCAG 2.0:** Introduce principle of P.O.U.R. and target all digital content (HTML, eBook, presentation, mobile app, Word Docs, Spreadsheet. etc.) Also categorize the guideline as
    
    ```jsx
    Principles > Guidelines > Success Criteria
    ```
    
- **WCAG 2.1:** Build on 2.0 and is a superset of WCAG 2.0 (Most law and regulation reference to this). Has a total of 78 Success Criteria
    
- **WCAG 2.2:** Current Official Recommendation; superset of WCAG 2.0 and 2.1. Trying to filling gap of the technology that was developed over a decade. Has total of 87 SCs
    
- **WCAG 3.0:** Extend to more than just web content that is Desktop, mobile app, IoT, wearable, etc. Currently an incomplete draft and will overhaul WCAG 2 in some years to come.
    

<aside> 💡

Note

WCAG 2.2 was built on top of WCAG 2.1 and WCAG 2.0 meaning that complying to WCAG 2.2 would conform to 2.0 and 2.1 as well.

</aside>

From the total of 87 SCs across the guidelines in the WCAG 2.2 standard, it is then sectioned into 3 levels: A, AA, and AAA.

AA is what rule and regulation mostly asked for, but AAA should be used whenever we can (even though it’s harder)
