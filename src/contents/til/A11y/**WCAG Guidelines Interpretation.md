Conforming to WCAG does not guarantee content is accessible. You can be “technically” conform to WCAG but still not inclusive to the user. This is an example of how the application can be compliant but still not inclusive or usable by some group of people.

![image.png|96](attachment:b0de7c18-cb7f-4db6-b027-82b21e8e01ba:image.png)

What about people who doesn’t want to talk on a phone? Deaf and hard of hearing? Providing text option is outside of the WCAG guideline but still beneficial nonetheless.

WCAG is not a checklist it’s a guideline to come up with a better solution and we can only be sure that things are accessible and usable only when by observing people succeed using it. Accessibility checking tool is just a simple check, use it to plan your accessibility plan instead.

> Meet the minimum standards. Do your WCAG checks. But don’t pat yourself on the back for being compliant. Accessibility is about making things work for people. It’s not passing an assessment or ticking a box to say you meet a standard. It’s about people and their needs just like any other part of user centred design. Do legal compliance as the absolute minimum, but always go further!
> 
> — Craig Abbott, Head of Accessibility at DWP Digital
